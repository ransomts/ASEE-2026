#+title: Vectorized Interdisciplinary Overlap between STEM Domains

* Intro & Context

** Multidisciplinary Motivation

- Students exercise /agency/ in course selection beyond their declared major
- Cross-departmental enrollment signals overlap between programs
- NSF identifies convergence research as a top 10 BIG Idea: grand challenges "will not be solved by one discipline alone"
- Interdisciplinary programs can broaden participation for students with marginalized identities

\note{Frame the problem: students are already interdisciplinary in their behavior---we want to measure and characterize that behavior structurally.}

** What is MIDFIELD?

- Multiple-Institution Database for Investigating Engineering Longitudinal Development
- Academic records of 98,000+ students from 18 US universities
- Data from 2000 onward to reflect contemporary curricula
- Contains: degree records, course enrollment, grades, demographics

\note{Emphasize that MIDFIELD has been used primarily for student persistence and migration studies. We are using it differently---to characterize relationships between programs rather than student pathways.}

** Contribution to the Field

- Shift unit of analysis from /student pathways/ to /program relationships/
- Student enrollment as expression of agency and interdisciplinary interest
- Complementary similarity metrics applied to enrollment vectors
- Define three interpretable relationship types:
  - *Nested*: one program's coursework is largely contained within another
  - *Parallel*: shared base, divergent specialization
  - *Convergent*: deep overlap in both general and specialized content
- Extend curricular analytics /across/ disciplinary boundaries

\note{Prior MIDFIELD work focuses on where students go. We focus on how programs relate to each other, using student behavior as the evidence.}

** Research Question

#+begin_export latex
\begin{center}
\Large\itshape
RQ: What types of curricular relationships have emerged between undergraduate STEM programs as evidenced by student enrollment patterns?
\end{center}
#+end_export

\note{Single research question. The methodology section explains how we operationalize "types of curricular relationships."}

* Methodology

** From Student Records to Program Vectors

1. Identify all courses taken by students completing each CIP code
2. Count course frequencies across all graduates of that major
3. Result: a high-dimensional /enrollment vector/ per program
4. Apply similarity metrics to compare vectors pairwise

\note{Key insight: we go beyond binary course presence (which only enables Jaccard) to frequency data, which enables the full suite of metrics.}

** Why Multiple Metrics?

- A single metric cannot distinguish:
  - Broad gen-ed overlap vs. specialized content overlap
  - Subset containment vs. symmetric overlap
  - Expected vs. statistically surprising co-enrollment

\note{This is the methodological argument of the paper---no single number tells the whole story. Each metric answers a different question about the relationship.}

*** Formula                                                           :BMCOL:
:PROPERTIES:
:BEAMER_col: 0.5
:END:

$$J(A,B) = \frac{|A \cap B|}{|A \cup B|}$$

/How much total curricular territory do two programs share?/

- Treats every course equally
- Diluted by rare electives in large programs

\note{Jaccard is our baseline---simple set overlap. The Venn diagram shows that the denominator is the full union, so programs with large elective sets get lower scores even if their cores overlap.}

*** Formula                                                           :BMCOL:
:PROPERTIES:
:BEAMER_col: 0.5

Upweights courses unique to few programs; downweights ubiquitous ones

/Do two programs share identity-defining coursework?/

- Resistant to program size differences
- Isolates specialized content from gen-ed noise

\note{TF-IDF is the key complement to Jaccard. The diagram shows that shared gen-ed courses (gray, low weight) are ignored while shared specialized courses (colored, high weight) drive the score.}

#+end_export

*** Formula                                                           :BMCOL:
:PROPERTIES:
:BEAMER_col: 0.5
:END:

$$\text{Overlap}(A,B) = \frac{|A \cap B|}{\min(|A|, |B|)}$$

/Is one program's curriculum largely a subset of another's?/

- Divides by the /smaller/ set
- High Overlap + Low Jaccard = *nested*

\note{The diagram shows IT almost entirely inside CS. Overlap catches this because it divides by the smaller set (IT). Jaccard misses it because the union (all of CS) is so much larger.}

*** Formula                                                           :BMCOL:
:PROPERTIES:
:BEAMER_col: 0.5
:END:

$$\text{PMI}(m,c) = \log\frac{p(m,c)}{p(m)\,p(c)}$$

/Do two programs share statistically surprising courses?/

- Compares observed co-occurrence to expected
- High PMI = courses that "belong together"

\note{PMI detects courses that co-occur in two programs far more than chance predicts. The left panel shows uniform distribution (what we'd expect if independent). The right shows clustering---courses that concentrate in specific program pairs have high PMI.}

** Kulczynski (Mutual Resemblance)

\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.8]
  % Large program
  \fill[C0!20] (-0.3,0) circle (1.7);
  % Small program
  \fill[C4!20] (1.5,0) circle (0.9);
  % Intersection

  \draw[thick,C4] (1.5,0) circle (0.9);
  \node[C0,font=\small\bfseries] at (-1.3,1.1) {MechE};
  \node[C4,font=\small\bfseries] at (2.0,0.7) {Aero};
  % Two arrows showing both perspectives
  \draw[->,thick,C0] (0.8,-1.8) -- (-0.3,-1.8) node[midway,below,font=\tiny] {$\frac{|A\cap B|}{|A|}$};
  \draw[->,thick,C4] (0.8,-2.4) -- (1.8,-2.4) node[midway,below,font=\tiny] {$\frac{|A\cap B|}{|B|}$};
  \node[font=\tiny,align=center] at (0.5,-3.2) {Average both\\perspectives};
\end{tikzpicture}
#+end_export

*** Formula                                                           :BMCOL:
:PROPERTIES:
:BEAMER_col: 0.5
:END:

$$\text{Kulczynski}(A,B) = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{|A \cap B|}{|A|} + \frac{|A \cap B|}{|B|}\right)$$

/How well would a student from one program fit in the other?/

- Averages overlap from /each/ perspective
- Robust for large vs. small programs

\note{Kulczynski is important because MIDFIELD has huge enrollment imbalances. The diagram shows that it averages two different views: what fraction of MechE is shared, and what fraction of Aero is shared. This is fairer than using just one denominator.}

** Relationship Taxonomy

#+CAPTION: Metric signatures for three curricular relationship types
#+ATTR_LATEX: :align lccccl :booktabs t
| Type       | Jaccard | TF-IDF | Overlap | PMI  | Example                |
|------------+---------+--------+---------+------+------------------------|
| Nested     | Low     | Low    | High    | Var. | IT \subset CS          |
| Parallel   | Med     | Low    | Med     | Low  | EE \parallel Civil Eng |
| Convergent | High    | High   | High    | High | Aero \approx Ind Eng   |

- The /pattern/ across metrics defines the relationship, not any single score
- Relative values within a domain matter more than absolute percentages

\note{This table is the core framework. Walk through each row: nested = high overlap but low Jaccard means containment. Parallel = shared base, divergent specialization. Convergent = high everything means deep structural alignment.}

* Wrap Up

** Key Takeaways
:PROPERTIES:
:BEAMER_opt: shrink=10
:END:

- STEM domains are interdisciplinary in /structurally different ways/
  - *Engineering*: parallel (shared core, divergent specialization)
  - *Computing*: nested hierarchies from CS
  - *Sciences*: cross-boundary clusters
- One-size-fits-all policy won't work

\note{Synthesis slide. The structural difference between domains is the main takeaway. The Aerospace--Industrial convergence is invisible to departmental taxonomy---multi-metric analysis is needed.}

** Practical Implications

- *Nested* (IT \subset CS): guide students toward parent field's electives
- *Parallel* (engineering): coordinate shared prerequisites
- *Convergent* (Aero \approx Ind Eng): cross-listed courses, certificates, dual degrees

\note{Give the audience something actionable. These aren't just academic categories---they suggest specific interventions at the advising, departmental, and university level.}

** Future Work
:PROPERTIES:
:BEAMER_opt: shrink=10
:END:

- Disaggregate required vs. elective coursework
- Time series: are programs converging or diverging?
- Institutional disaggregation across universities
- Validate against known interdisciplinary programs

\note{Acknowledge limitations honestly. The biggest one is that we can't currently separate required from elective enrollment.}
